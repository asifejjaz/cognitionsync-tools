# Traffic Tool Portfolio Research

Research date: 8 October 2026
Prepared for: a developer with an existing VM, seeking an advertising-funded portfolio of useful websites and mobile apps.

## Recommendation

Start with one focused website for preparing images and documents: compress an image to a requested size, resize it, convert image formats, assemble photos into a PDF, merge/split PDFs, and add a visible signature. Expand into document conversion after the first tools work reliably and attract real users.

A second product can specialize in document-to-Markdown workflows for developers, researchers, and documentation teams. A third can offer calculators for a specific audience, such as freelancers, students, or construction estimators. Develop native mobile apps when repeat use demonstrates a reason to install one.

This is a portfolio plan. The 120 candidates below are a backlog, not a recommendation to launch 120 products simultaneously. Publishing more tools does not by itself create traffic. A VM supplies hosting capacity; distribution, differentiation, conversion quality, and operating margins determine the business.

Assumptions: global English audience initially; responsive website first; existing VM available later; no existing audience, advertising account approval, paid keyword account, or distribution partnership assumed. VM specifications and bandwidth allowance are unknown.

## Evidence And Limits

I reviewed public competitor traffic pages, current competing tool offerings, Google publisher policies, app store listings, and primary documentation for conversion technology.

The comparable website benchmark is August 2026, the latest common month exposed by the Semrush pages reviewed. These are modeled worldwide monthly visits across the entire domain, not unique people, individual tool demand, audited analytics, or forecasts for a new entrant. Some sites contain many products or substantial editorial content. Do not sum these visits into an addressable market: users and sessions overlap across competitors.

[Semrush explains that Traffic Analytics estimates traffic using clickstream data](https://www.semrush.com/kb/858-traffic-numbers-in-semrush). The organic search estimate in its SEO reports is a different metric. This report uses total visits for the benchmark table.

I cross-checked iLovePDF on [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/ilovepdf.com/), which placed it at global rank 113 for September 2026. That supports the broad conclusion that this is a large category. Differences in period, provider, geography, and estimation method make exact provider comparisons inappropriate.

The demand and competition labels below are research judgments. This research does not include a complete paid keyword export or numerical SEO difficulty scores. Exact demand for most individual ideas remains unverified. Mobile download counts are cumulative install bands, not monthly active users.

## Ideas To Deprioritize

### Broad YouTube And Social Video Downloaders

There is observable demand: the ssyoutube traffic benchmark above is 14.38 million modeled August visits. I would not make unrestricted platform downloading the foundation of a portfolio depending on Google advertising.

[YouTube's terms](https://www.youtube.com/static?template=terms) restrict downloading or other use except when authorized by the service or with the required written permissions. [Google's publisher copyright policy](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10402772?hl=en) prohibits infringing content. This is not a claim that every possible download is unlawful; permissions, method, jurisdiction, and the actual product matter.

In addition to policy exposure, such a service depends on third-party changes and can incur substantial bandwidth and abuse costs. Owning the video does not automatically remove every platform-terms issue.

A cleaner initial creator product is conversion, trimming, compression, and audio extraction from files users supply. That lets us validate a useful workflow without relying on access to another platform.

### Other Poor Initial Fits

- Temporary email inboxes: abuse and deliverability work; AdSense placement rules restrict ads where emails/private communications are the primary focus.
- Generic public URL shorteners or file-sharing sites: abuse handling and ongoing storage/egress; business subscribers are often a more suitable funding source.
- Unlimited free AI writing, image generation, transcription, or background removal: usage costs can grow faster than advertising income.
- Generic news/weather/stock-price aggregators: licensing, update reliability, copied-content issues, and strong incumbents.
- Medical, legal, and tax advice generators: subject expertise and ongoing validation required; country-specific calculators also need rule maintenance.
- A portal full of cloned or minimally changed tool pages: weak differentiation and search/publisher-content risks.
- "AI detector" or resume tools making guaranteed accuracy or hiring claims: difficult claims to substantiate.

These judgments describe initial business fit, not a prohibition on building legitimate products in these areas.

## Advertising And Revenue

For websites, investigate AdSense after the product and content are complete enough for review. For native mobile apps, [AdMob](https://admob.google.com/home/) is Google's relevant app advertising product. Website approval does not establish approval for every app or future site.

Google does not promise approval or a fixed earning rate. [Its low-value-content policy](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11112688?hl=en) limits ads on screens without sufficient publisher value. A functional tool is a useful starting point; add original examples, supported-format details, troubleshooting, verified methods, and clear ownership/contact/privacy information where they serve users. There is no documented universal "30 articles" or fixed visitor threshold that guarantees approval.

[Ad placement rules](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1346295?hl=en) prohibit encouraging accidental clicks and disguising ads as navigation or download links. Keep download, conversion, crop, and signature controls clearly separated from advertising. Do not create forced advertising countdowns before downloads. These rules also restrict AdSense distribution through software such as browser extensions and desktop applications.

For personalized ads to visitors in the EEA, UK, and Switzerland, [Google specifies certified consent-management requirements](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554116?hl=en). Implement the applicable consent flow when we integrate ads.

### Illustrative Economics

[Google defines page RPM](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/112030?hl=en) using earnings per thousand page views. It is different from ad impression RPM and visitor/session counts.

Monthly gross ad revenue = monthly page views / 1,000 x realized page RPM.

The following values are sensitivity scenarios, not researched niche RPM benchmarks or income forecasts. Actual RPM can fall outside this range, including below USD 1.

| Monthly page views | At USD 1 page RPM | At USD 3 page RPM | At USD 8 page RPM |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 50,000 | USD 50 | USD 150 | USD 400 |
| 100,000 | USD 100 | USD 300 | USD 800 |
| 500,000 | USD 500 | USD 1,500 | USD 4,000 |
| 1,000,000 | USD 1,000 | USD 3,000 | USD 8,000 |

At an assumed USD 3 page RPM, USD 1,000 gross per month requires roughly 333,334 page views. USD 3,000 requires one million page views. Costs and tax are still to be deducted.

Realized results depend on audience country, advertisers, device mix, consent, ad visibility, coverage, seasonality, and traffic quality. Search CPC or an incumbent's traffic cannot establish our future RPM. Track valid human usage, not bot requests or raw CDN hits.

Net operating income = ad revenue + optional paid revenue - VM/CDN/storage/bandwidth/processing/monitoring/support costs. Keep bulk processing, larger files, expensive AI work, saved workflows, and API access available as possible paid features once users show demand.

Offline use of a PWA may be useful to users, but it should not be counted as guaranteed advertising revenue.

## VM And Scaling Plan

Prefer local processing for supported image transformations, basic PDF operations, text cleanup, and data conversion. The VM can serve the application and support conversions that genuinely need server resources. A CDN can cache public static assets.

[PDF-lib documents browser support and page operations](https://pdf-lib.js.org/), which supports the feasibility of local merge/split/basic PDF manipulation. This does not mean it provides faithful Word conversion, advanced PDF compression, or certificate signing.

[Mammoth](https://github.com/mwilliamson/mammoth.js) converts DOCX structure into HTML and supports browser use. A Markdown product still needs additional conversion, asset handling, output checks, and safe preview rendering. Conversion quality must be tested against actual documents.

[Gotenberg's LibreOffice route](https://gotenberg.dev/docs/convert-with-libreoffice/convert-to-pdf) supports office-to-PDF conversion. Use isolated queued conversion workers, maintained fonts, file/time limits, and cleanup for server jobs. Do not promise identical Microsoft Word rendering for every document.

A visible drawn signature is different from a certificate-based digital signature. [Adobe explains certificate-based digital IDs](https://helpx.adobe.com/sign/web/settings-configuration/digital-signatures/overview.html). Start with a clearly named fill/sign workflow and validate more formal signing requirements separately.

For a public conversion service, our deployment should include upload size limits, bounded processing, automatic deletion, isolated workers, rate controls, monitoring, and protection against arbitrary server-side URL fetches. These protect service availability and users' files; they are also operating costs.

Bandwidth example: 100,000 server conversions each delivering an average 10 MB file produce about 1 TB of download traffic. One million produce about 10 TB. These decimal totals exclude static assets, retries, and other traffic. Local file processing avoids sending those source/output files through the VM.

CPU example: 100,000 jobs using ten CPU-seconds each consume about 277.8 core-hours, before overhead. Average load hides peaks, so worker capacity requires measurement.

A single VM may be sufficient to launch. It is not a guarantee of arbitrary heavy traffic: browser workload, concurrency, core count, RAM, file sizes, bandwidth caps, and failure tolerance determine the limit. CDN delivery and separate workers/storage are options as usage grows.

## Concrete First Release

Build one image/document product with these twelve workflow areas:

1. Image compression to a requested KB limit.
2. Image resize/crop.
3. HEIC conversion.
4. WebP conversion.
5. Photo/signature upload preparation.
6. JPG/PNG to PDF.
7. Merge PDFs.
8. Split PDFs.
9. Rotate/reorder PDF pages.
10. Add a visible signature and text to PDF.
11. DOCX to Markdown.
12. Markdown to PDF.

Treat this as a staged release: the first eight working tools can go live while the remaining four are completed and checked. Add Word-to-PDF when the server conversion service and representative font/layout tests are ready.

Shared components: file import, preview, batch queue, progress/errors, format validation, ZIP/download handling, size reporting, and mobile interactions. Each route should complete a useful task.

The first acquisition strategy should focus on actual workflows, such as "reduce application photo to 50 KB and 200 x 230 pixels" or "convert a DOCX with tables and images to a Markdown ZIP". These example phrases are hypotheses for keyword validation, not measured opportunities.

## Validation And Launch Sequence

### Stage 1: Validate The First Audience

- Pick one initial user group: application-form users, office workers, or documentation teams.
- Export relevant keyword ideas for a chosen country and language, then inspect the actual search results.
- Test conversion failure cases using a representative corpus of files with permission to use them.
- Choose a clear product advantage we can demonstrate with before/after examples.

[Keyword Planner defines monthly search counts according to the selected location and period](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575?hl=en-2). Its advertising competition metric is not organic ranking difficulty. [Google Trends is normalized to a 0-100 scale](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533?hl=en-AU); it is useful for direction and seasonality, not exact monthly volumes. I did not retrieve live Trends time-series data for this report.

### Stage 2: Build And Launch A Cohesive Toolkit

Release useful routes with original examples, accurate limitations, clear file handling, and working mobile interfaces. Connect Search Console and usage analytics. Track successful operations, error rate, referral source, device, repeat use, and server cost per operation.

Do not start with thousands of near-identical size/format pages. Useful presets can be part of a real tool; separate pages need distinct user value. [Google's search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) address doorway pages and scaled low-value content.

### Stage 3: Acquire Users

- Publish original comparisons and troubleshooting built from our test results.
- Demonstrate actual workflows to communities relevant to the chosen audience, following their posting rules.
- Offer export templates, worked examples, and useful downloadable samples.
- Seek voluntary mentions from documentation, education, and workflow sites.
- Add properly localized pages only when we can maintain the tools and content.

Simple unit/date questions can often be answered without visiting a site. As a product judgment, tasks that require manipulating a user's file are less easily completed by a static search answer. That makes them a sensible initial focus, though it does not guarantee rankings.

### Stage 4: Measure Before Expanding

Use the first roughly 90 days to learn which tasks users complete and return to. It is a measurement window, not a promise of ranking, approval, or earnings within 90 days.

Expand when one cluster shows growing relevant impressions, successful usage, manageable error/support load, and acceptable operating economics. Rework or stop ideas with little demand, poor retention, or processing costs that exceed plausible revenue.

Develop a second brand/site after the first has a proven acquisition path. Build a mobile app when offline capture, frequent use, saved projects, or native sharing justify installation.

## Practical Decision

My recommended first business is a useful image/PDF preparation website with most operations performed locally. My recommended second is a document/Markdown conversion workbench with demonstrably better handling of tables, images, and batch migration. My recommended third is a calculator product for one specific audience.

These can become assets with recurring revenue. They will still need maintenance, conversion fixes, security updates, content work, distribution, and advertising-policy checks. Heavy traffic and passive income are outcomes to earn and measure, not properties supplied by a large backlog or a VM.

The next build brief should specify the first audience, product/domain name, first eight routes, supported file limits, and the quality tests. VM deployment details can be settled after its CPU, RAM, disk, bandwidth allowance, OS, and provider are available.
